## What happened

I kicked things off by uploading an initial draft of the Team Charter so we'd have something in the repo to work from right away, covering the basics the brief asked for: communication, deadlines, decision-making, conflict resolution, and our commit convention.

## What went well

- Getting a first draft into the repo quickly meant the team had something concrete to react to instead of starting from a blank page.
- Handing off to Ann's rewrite instead of insisting on my own version was the right call — it became a genuinely better, more complete charter than my draft was.
- The small fixes (name, comms section, sign-off) were quick and didn't need back-and-forth.

## What was harder than expected

- Having two people start a charter at roughly the same time meant a bit of wasted effort on my end — I should have checked in before uploading a full draft rather than assuming I'd own that piece.
- Deciding to delete my own content rather than merge the two versions felt like the cleaner move, but it meant my first commit didn't end up contributing much to the final document.

## Self-assessment

I was fast to act but not great at coordinating first. The actual outcome was fine because Ann's version was solid and I didn't get precious about my draft, but going forward I'd rather spend two minutes confirming who's doing what before putting something into the repo.
