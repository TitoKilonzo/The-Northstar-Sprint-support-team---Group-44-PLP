# Day 1 Reflection — August 13

## What happened

I took the Team Lead role and helped coordinate the initial Northstar Support MVP setup. The main focus was getting the team's working process established before development moved forward.

I created the initial Team Charter and worked through the team's communication, availability, task ownership, priorities, Definition of Done, board updates, conflict resolution, escalation process, and commit conventions. The Charter was then reviewed and acknowledged by the team members.

I also helped establish the GitHub repository/project structure and worked with the team during our Google Meet sessions to understand the MVP requirements and decide what we would build. We focused our scope on the two ticket categories required for the MVP: **order status and returns/refunds**.

The audit history shows my contributions that day included:

- `966ca5d` — Create team charter for Northstar Support MVP
- `a8676cd` — Fix formatting in Team Charter title
- `b893736` — Team acknowledgement

The team also made subsequent Charter updates and acknowledgements.

## What went well

- I was able to take responsibility for coordinating the team and getting the working agreement established.
- We clearly identified the two MVP areas we needed to deliver instead of trying to build all three support categories.
- I was comfortable handling the documentation and organizing the team's expectations around communication and accountability.
- Working together on Google Meet helped us understand each other's strengths and limitations, especially because the team had different levels of technical experience.

## What was harder than expected

- Getting five people to consistently participate was more difficult than expected. Although everyone acknowledged the Charter, not everyone was consistently available or contributing.
- I initially expected the work to be divided cleanly between individual members, but as the sprint progressed, we had to adapt because some members were not able to contribute at the expected level.
- Balancing the Team Lead responsibility with learning and contributing technically was challenging.

## Self-assessment

I think I handled the coordination and setup responsibilities well, but I learned that being a Team Lead also means adapting when the original plan does not work. I needed to become more hands-on technically while still keeping the project moving.
