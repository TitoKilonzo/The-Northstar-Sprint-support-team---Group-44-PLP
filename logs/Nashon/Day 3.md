# Day 3 Reflection — August 15

## What happened

The team shifted into high gear with active coding, debugging, and harmonizing the frontend, backend, and API frameworks. Our Google Meet sessions became intense troubleshooting marathons as we worked to get all the moving parts of the Northstar Support MVP to communicate with each other correctly.

This was the day where I started to feel a slight shift in my own comprehension. Having spent Day 2 actively listening and asking basic questions, I began to recognize patterns and connect the dots between the UI, the API routes, and the database logic. I was able to move beyond pure observation and actually contribute to the discussion—specifically around the **return eligibility criteria**. I offered comments and suggestions on the conditions that should determine whether a customer qualifies for a return, helping to refine the logic from a user-experience perspective.

While the team continued handling the heavy lifting of the code integration, I made a point to stay engaged, offer input where I could, and learn by watching how the team resolved conflicts between the different frameworks.

---

## What went well

- I successfully transitioned from being a silent observer to an active participant in the technical discussion, even if my contributions were small.
- My comments on the return eligibility criteria were well-received, and I could see how my input helped clarify the business logic behind the feature.
- The team's accommodating and patient attitude made it safe for me to speak up, even when I wasn't 100% sure of myself. They never made me feel like my questions or suggestions were unwelcome.
- I gained a much clearer understanding of how the frontend, API, and database interact—concepts that felt completely abstract just a day ago are now starting to feel tangible.
- I continued to build trust with my teammates by showing up consistently and demonstrating genuine effort to learn and engage.

---

## What was harder than expected

- Despite my growing understanding, I still felt very "green" compared to my colleagues. The speed at which they diagnosed and fixed issues was far beyond my current capability, and I often found myself struggling to keep up with the rapid-fire problem-solving.
- Some tasks were assigned to me with the hope that I could take them on, but I had to honestly admit that I could not deliver them effectively. The technical complexity was still too high for my current skill level, and I felt a bit frustrated that I couldn't pull my weight in that way.
- The sheer volume of interconnected issues—a bug in the API affecting the frontend, or a database mismatch breaking the user flow—made it difficult for me to trace cause and effect without help.
- It was hard to balance the desire to contribute more with the reality of my limitations. I had to remind myself that learning takes time.

---

## Self-assessment

Day 3 was a milestone for me, not because I wrote a lot of code, but because I was able to **shift from passive listening to active contribution**. Offering meaningful comments on the return eligibility criteria showed me that my background (even non-technical) brings value—I can think about user logic and business rules in ways that complement the team's technical expertise.

However, I also had to confront the reality that I am still very early in my learning journey. Not being able to deliver on assigned tasks was a humbling experience, but my teammates' accommodating response reassured me that growth is a process, not a race. I am no longer just "getting lost"; I am now starting to "get it" in small pieces. My goal moving forward is to continue building on this momentum, work on my technical skills independently, and gradually increase the scope of what I can reliably deliver for the team.
