
# Day 2 Reflection — August 14

## What happened

The team moved deeper into the MVP development, and I joined the Google Meet session ready to collaborate alongside my colleagues. At the start of the call, I openly explained my non-technical background and acknowledged that this would naturally limit my hands-on contribution to the actual coding and repository work.

Rather than stepping back, I chose to lean into the process differently. I spent the majority of the session actively listening to the discussions, observing how the team approached problems, and asking clarifying questions whenever I found myself getting lost. I focused on understanding the *flow* of the project—how the frontend connected to the backend, what the API routes were handling, and how the team was structuring their work around the order status and returns/refunds features.

Even without writing code, I made sure to stay present, take notes, and validate my understanding by repeating key points back to the team to confirm I was following along correctly.

---

## What went well

- The team was incredibly supportive and patient when I asked questions, which made me feel comfortable admitting when I didn't understand something.
- By asking fundamental "why" and "how" questions, I was able to offer a fresh perspective—sometimes helping the team clarify their own thinking by forcing them to explain decisions in plain language.
- I successfully built on the rapport I established on Day 1, and my teammates began to see me as someone who brings value through communication and clarity, not just code.
- I remained fully engaged throughout the session, maintaining focus even when the technical discussions became dense and unfamiliar.
- I walked away with a much clearer mental model of the project's architecture and team workflow than I had at the start of the day.

---

## What was harder than expected

- Catching up with colleagues who had more technical experience was significantly harder than I anticipated. Most of the terminology, tools (like GitHub workflows), and debugging logic were concepts I was encountering for the first time.
- It was challenging to follow the conversation when the team switched rapidly between frontend issues, database queries, and integration problems. I often felt like I was piecing together fragments of a larger puzzle.
- There were moments when I felt imposter syndrome creeping in, especially when the team was deep in technical troubleshooting and I couldn't actively contribute to solving the issue.
- Balancing the desire to help with the reality of my current skill level required humility and patience.

---

## Self-assessment

While my technical contribution to the MVP on Day 2 was minimal, I view this as a successful and necessary step in my learning journey. I took ownership of my knowledge gap by being transparent with my team and proactive in my listening. Instead of pretending to understand or disengaging, I asked thoughtful questions that helped solidify my own understanding and occasionally prompted the team to consider things from a different angle.

I recognize that contribution to a project is not limited to writing code—being an engaged listener, a clear communicator, and someone who asks the right questions is equally valuable, especially in the early stages of team formation. I am proud that I stayed curious, remained humble, and continued building trust with my teammates. This foundation will serve me well as I continue to learn and gradually take on more active technical responsibilities in the days ahead.
